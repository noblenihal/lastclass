3. DESCRIBE THE CHANGES/UPDATES MADE IN THE DEPLOYED VERSION
   [paste-ready · 968/1024 chars]
================================================================
LastClass teaches you any topic in five levels, then makes you teach it back to a class that asks you questions.

Tell it what you want to learn and how deep to go — the same topic taught to a ten-year-old and to a university student becomes a genuinely different course, not the same one reworded. You can also paste something you've written about it, and it works out what you already understand.

Level 1, you watch it explained on a whiteboard, with drawings appearing as they're mentioned. Level 2, it's compared to a hobby you already know well. Level 3, you close your eyes and build a picture of it in your head. Level 4, you actually do it — photograph your attempt, or find the mistake in a broken example.

Level 5 is the real test. You explain the topic to five students, and they ask about exactly the parts you glossed over. Answer vaguely and they press harder. Skip a question and that hand stays up.

There's no quiz anywhere. Teaching it is the test.

4. GEN AI SERVICES UTILISED, AND WHERE
   [paste-ready · 1017/1024 chars]
================================================================
Google Gemini only. Every call runs server-side in a Next.js route handler; the key is an encrypted env var and never reaches the browser.

gemini-3.7-flash (schema-constrained JSON + vision):
- /api/curriculum - concept map, prerequisites, theory/practical call, level titles, level detection from your writing
- /api/lesson - builds levels 1-4
- /api/lesson/steer - adjusts your mental image mid-visualisation
- /api/concept - each concept's brief and every "go deeper" layer
- /api/classroom/doubts - the questions the class raises
- /api/classroom/judge - judges answers in character, returns follow-ups
- /api/classroom/mentor - the Master's hint
- /api/assess - grades photographed work or a diagnosis

gemini-3.1-flash-tts-preview:
- /api/tts - every character voice and all narration

gemini-3.1-flash-image:
- /api/room - the classroom, dressed for your subject
- /api/lesson/sketch - the chalk drawings on the board
- build time - the students' sprite sheets

Nothing is mocked. Every output is a live call.

================================================================
ERROR HANDLING, GUARDRAILS AND ACCESSIBILITY
================================================================
Failures are typed, not generic. lib/gemini.ts inspects promptFeedback,
finishReason and HTTP status and raises a GeminiError carrying a kind
(blocked / recitation / rate_limited / timeout / config / unavailable), a
message written for the learner, and a retryable flag. Safety blocks name the
category that tripped where the API reports it.

Three verdicts on intake. TEACHABLE builds the course. DECLINED refuses on
policy grounds with a stated reason and no retry offered, because retrying a
policy refusal cannot succeed. UNINTELLIGIBLE rejects input that is not a
recognisable subject — keyboard mashing — because inventing a plausible course
from gibberish would be fabricating content. A merely vague subject still
builds.

The rule the prompt enforces: refuse on what the learner asks you to enable,
never on whether the subject is uncomfortable. Verified — meth synthesis is
declined; amphetamine pharmacology and the Holocaust both build normally.

Voice, the classroom backdrop and the chalk sketches all fail soft. Losing any
of them never blocks a lesson.

Accessibility: every Framer Motion loop is gated on useReducedMotion, because
the CSS prefers-reduced-motion block only ever reached CSS animations while
waving hands and pulsing rings ran regardless. Live regions announce
everything that arrives from a network call. The concept drawer is a real
modal with focus moved in, focus returned on close, and background scroll
locked. Quiz options are a radiogroup; diagrams carry descriptions; form hints
are associated via aria-describedby; there is a skip link and a main landmark
on every page.

Design system: four learner-selectable themes differing on paper band and
accent hue, defaulting to light. Every colour is a named token — the codebase
contains no raw colour values at the point of use.
